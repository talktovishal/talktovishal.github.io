Chapter 5

Transformers: the architecture inside today’s LLMs, one piece at a time.

Time to open up the Transformer, the architecture inside GPT-2 and nearly every LLM you’ve heard of. We’ll meet its parts in the order your text flows through them: tokens, token and position embeddings, attention (queries, keys and values, across many heads), the feed-forward network, the stack of blocks, and the output that turns it all into a guess. And we won’t use a toy to do it. We’ll follow one real prediction by GPT-2 small (12 blocks, 12 attention heads in each block, 768 numbers for every token) through every part, all the way to brand-new text. Every number on this page comes from one saved run of that model, so there’s nothing to download.

Before we dive in, a few prerequisites are in order.

  • Next-token prediction: a language model’s whole job is to give every possible next token a probability.
  • Tokens and token IDs and embeddings: text becomes pieces, and each piece’s ID picks a row of numbers.
  • Dot products and softmax: multiply two lists and add them up, then turn scores into probabilities.
  • Neural-network layers: weights, biases, and a bend between layers.

What you do not need yet: how these models are trained at huge scale, or the tricks that make them fast. Those come in later chapters.

This chapter is my attempt to slow the machinery down until you can reconstruct it yourself. These sources go further; they are not substitutes for a missing step in the explanation above.

  • Radford et al., Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners: the GPT-2 paper. Every number in this chapter comes from the GPT-2 small checkpoint (Hugging Face revision 607a30d, license).
  • Umar Jamil, Attention Is All You Need: our primary completeness companion. Start at notation (09:02), attention (20:08), multihead (28:30), normalization (37:55), decoder (40:13), training (44:59), or inference (52:09). Companion diagrams and notes.
  • Vaswani et al., Attention Is All You Need: the original encoder-decoder architecture, post-norm order, and scaled dot-product attention.
  • 3Blue1Brown's Transformer overview, attention, and MLPs: visual intuition for contextual vectors and the two major branches.
  • Karpathy, Let's build GPT, with executable code: a compact pre-norm decoder you can actually train.
  • The Annotated Transformer: tensor shapes, masks, and a runnable implementation. Its normalization order differs from the original diagram it quotes.
  • The Illustrated Transformer and Hands-On Large Language Models: companion explanations and model inspection.
  • Jurafsky and Martin, Speech and Language Processing: broader language-model and Transformer foundations.
  • Transformer Explainer and Brendan Bycroft's LLM visualization: larger interactive views. Their model exports and numerical examples differ from ours.
  • Lost in the Middle: why a large context window and reliable use of all its contents are different claims.
